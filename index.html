Weather, Policy, and Topic Modeling of 311 Reports on Homelessness in San Diego

A data-driven look at how environmental conditions and municipal policy intersect with the topics reported in 311 calls, and how these patterns relate to encampment migration across the city.

Problem Statement

San Diego faces recurring shifts in the distribution and persistence of homeless encampments. While weather variability (heat, rain, cold snaps) and policy actions (ordinances, sweeps, service expansions) are suspected drivers, quantitative evidence linking these factors to the discourse found in public 311 reports and to observed encampment migration remains limited.

This project applies topic modeling to homelessness-related 311 requests to identify recurring themes, and examines their associations with local weather, policy timelines, and space-time movement of encampments. Our aim is to inform humane, effective, and climate-aware responses.

Understanding homelessness requires examining relationships among environmental pressures, policy shifts, and public behavior. The central research question guiding this project is:

How do weather events, policy interventions, and public sentiment collectively influence the movement and visibility of homeless encampments in San Diego?

Our sub-questions include:

  • Do encampment sweeps and policy actions correspond with spatial shifts in reported 311 call hotspots?
  • Do weather events, particularly heat waves and rainfall, affect the visibility or geographic clustering of outdoor encampments?
  • Does public sentiment expressed through 311 calls align with or predict periods of increased policy enforcement?

This investigation is crucial for creating humane and effective policy responses. Policies based on incomplete or misinterpreted data may displace vulnerable populations rather than address root causes. Integrating multiple datasets provides a more complete picture of the dynamics at play.
