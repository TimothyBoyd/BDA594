Weather, Policy, and Topic Modeling of 311 Reports on Homelessness in San Diego

A data-driven look at how environmental conditions and municipal policy intersect with the topics reported in 311 calls, and how these patterns relate to encampment migration across the city.

Problem Statement

Homelessness in San Diego is shaped by a complex mix of social, environmental, and policy forces. To better understand how unsheltered individuals move throughout the city, this project integrates three key data sources: community sentiment from 311 requests, long-term climate patterns, and geocoded policy actions such as encampment sweeps and shelter expansions.

Each dataset offers a different perspective on how homelessness evolves across time and space. It shows how residents perceive it, how weather affects where people can safely stay, and how policy decisions reshape the landscape of available options. By combining these insights, we identify the conditions that concentrate or disperse unsheltered populations and highlight how policies can either amplify or ease these dynamics.

The goal is to provide a more holistic, data-driven foundation for creating responsive and equitable strategies to address homelessness in San Diego.
