Encampment Migration

This page summarizes how encampment locations shifted over time in response to policy actions, community reporting patterns, and climate variability across San Diego.

Policy Impacts on Encampment Migration

Policy interventions—especially encampment sweeps and new regulations—were closely tied to movement of unsheltered populations. Sweeps in Downtown and Mid-City often pushed encampments into adjacent neighborhoods, where 311 activity rose after enforcement. In contrast, new shelters and safe-parking resources sometimes stabilized nearby encampment presence. Across years, policy decisions consistently shaped both the timing and direction of encampment relocation.

311 Hotspot Patterns and Migration

Spatial clustering of homelessness-related 311 calls revealed recurring migration corridors, particularly Downtown → Mid-City and Mission Bay → Pacific Beach. Topic-specific hotspots showed how different encampment types shifted: camping, obstruction, and sanitation-related reports signaled encampments moving into nearby residential or park areas, often following enforcement activity or seasonal pressure.

Climate Influences on Encampment Movement

Climate variability played a measurable role in encampment mobility. Hotter months aligned with increased outdoor encampment presence, especially downtown and near the coast where heat exposure is lower. Heavy rainfall corresponded with decreased visible encampments as people sought shelter or moved to covered areas, temporarily redistributing populations across the city. Seasonal climate patterns reliably influenced migration and outdoor sheltering.
