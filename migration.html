Encampment Migration

This page summarizes how encampment locations shifted over time in response to policy actions, community reporting patterns, and climate variability across San Diego.

Policy Impacts on Encampment Migration

Policy interventions, especially encampment sweeps and new regulations, were closely tied to movement of unsheltered populations. Sweeps in Downtown and Mid-City often pushed encampments into adjacent neighborhoods, where 311 activity rose after enforcement. New shelters and safe-parking resources sometimes stabilized encampment presence nearby.

311 Hotspot Patterns and Migration

Homelessness-related 311 calls revealed recurring migration corridors, particularly Downtown → Mid-City and Mission Bay → Pacific Beach. Topic-specific hotspots showed how different encampment types shifted into surrounding residential or park areas, often following enforcement activity or seasonal changes.

Climate Influences on Encampment Movement

Climate variability shaped when and where people could remain outdoors. Hotter months corresponded with increased outdoor encampment presence, especially downtown and near the coast. Heavy rainfall temporarily reduced visible encampments as individuals sought shelter or moved to covered areas.
