Team · Weather, Policy, and Topic Modeling of 311 Reports on Homelessness in San Diego

Team roles and responsibilities.

Team Roles and Responsibilities

Ashley Funk

  • Compiled homelessness-related policy events from government sources and credible news outlets.
  • Cataloged events such as encampment sweeps, shelter expansions/closures, ordinances, and new resources.
  • Categorized each policy event as location-specific, citywide, or statewide.
  • Geocoded policy events into spatial point data and symbolized them by governance scale.
  • Overlaid policy layers on 311 density data to examine relationships between policy actions and spatial shifts in homelessness.
  • Created annual policy event maps and integrated them into an interactive ArcGIS Experience with a clickable time-series.

Timothy Boyd

  • Downloaded, cleaned, and filtered the full 311 service request dataset.
  • Identified 8,200 homelessness-related reports and refined a high-signal subset for text analysis.
  • Applied FastTopic topic modeling to reveal dominant sub-themes in resident concerns.
  • Conducted kernel density estimation to map spatial hotspots across San Diego.
  • Created topic-specific KDE maps to examine how concerns vary geographically.
  • Built an interactive temporal heatmap to track changes in homelessness-related concerns over time.

Jonathan Siegel

  • Collected and processed climate datasets including GHCN-Daily, ERA5-Land, and Downtown Homeless Sleepers data.
  • Aggregated daily climate data into monthly averages and calculated key indicators such as maximum temperature, precipitation totals, and heat-day counts.
  • Standardized climate and homelessness variables using z-scores to enable direct comparison across time.
  • Merged climate metrics with monthly homelessness counts to analyze seasonal and environmental patterns.
  • Created time-series visualizations showing relationships between heat, precipitation, and unsheltered population trends.
  • Identified recurring seasonal patterns, including increased homelessness visibility during warm months and decreases during periods of heavy rainfall.
  • Documented data limitations such as missing months, climate dataset size constraints, and challenges with poorly documented environmental datasets.
